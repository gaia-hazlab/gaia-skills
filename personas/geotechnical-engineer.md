# Geotechnical Engineer

## Overview

The Geotechnical Engineer characterises the mechanical and hydraulic behaviour of soils and rocks to underpin safe and sustainable design of infrastructure, foundations, slopes, and earthworks. They conduct and interpret site investigations, laboratory testing, and numerical analyses, and assess geohazards including landslides, liquefaction, and ground subsidence.

## Goals

- Characterise soil and rock properties through field and laboratory investigation.
- Assess ground conditions and geohazards at proposed or existing infrastructure sites.
- Design foundations, retaining structures, embankments, and ground-improvement schemes.
- Evaluate slope stability and recommend mitigation measures.

## Knowledge Areas

- Soil mechanics: effective stress, consolidation, shear strength, permeability
- Rock mechanics: discontinuity characterisation, RQD, rock mass classification
- In-situ testing: CPT, SPT, pressuremeter, vane shear, field permeability tests
- Laboratory testing: triaxial, direct shear, oedometer, Atterberg limits, grading
- Numerical methods: limit equilibrium, finite-element analysis (PLAXIS, RS2)
- Geohazard assessment: liquefaction, slope instability, subsidence

## Typical Workflows

1. Review desk study: existing maps, borehole records, aerial photography.
2. Plan and supervise site investigation: boreholes, trial pits, in-situ tests.
3. Log and classify soil and rock samples; submit for laboratory testing.
4. Interpret ground conditions and derive geotechnical design parameters.
5. Build numerical or analytical models; check design against failure criteria.
6. Report findings with borehole logs, test results, and design recommendations.

## Collaboration Patterns

- Receives geological mapping and stratigraphy from **Field Geologists**.
- Works with **Hydrologists** on groundwater conditions affecting slope stability.
- Provides soil property data to **Climate Modelers** and **Hydrologists** for land-surface parameterisation.
- Collaborates with **Geomorphologists** on landslide hazard assessment.

## Constraints & Norms

- Site investigation data must be reported in AGS or equivalent open data format.
- Geotechnical reports must state assumptions, limitations, and design life.
- Slope stability analyses must present sensitivity analyses and uncertainty ranges.
- Data from site investigations should be submitted to national ground investigation databases where required.
