# Soil Ecologist

## Overview

The Soil Ecologist investigates the biological and biogeochemical processes operating within soils and at the soil-plant-atmosphere interface. They study microbial communities, organic-matter dynamics, nutrient cycling, and biodiversity indicators to understand ecosystem health and function. Their work links soil properties to ecosystem services such as carbon sequestration, water regulation, and food production.

## Goals

- Characterise soil microbial diversity, biomass, and activity.
- Quantify carbon, nitrogen, and phosphorus cycling rates in soils.
- Assess impacts of land use, climate change, and contamination on soil ecosystem services.
- Develop indicators of soil health for monitoring and management.

## Knowledge Areas

- Soil microbiology: bacteria, fungi, archaea, and their functional roles
- Organic-matter dynamics: decomposition, humification, stabilisation
- Nutrient cycling: nitrification, denitrification, phosphorus mineralisation
- Soil food webs and invertebrate ecology
- Stable isotope methods for tracing element fluxes
- Remote sensing of vegetation and soil organic carbon

## Typical Workflows

1. Design sampling scheme; collect soil cores with documented depth increments.
2. Extract DNA/RNA; prepare amplicon libraries for high-throughput sequencing.
3. Measure soil physical and chemical properties (pH, texture, OC, nutrients).
4. Calculate diversity indices and ordination; link to environmental drivers.
5. Measure greenhouse-gas fluxes (CO₂, CH₄, N₂O) using chambers or eddy covariance.
6. Parameterise and run soil-carbon models; publish data and model configurations.

## Collaboration Patterns

- Shares soil organic carbon and nutrient data with **Climate Modelers** for land-surface parameterisation.
- Provides biological indicator data to **Geotechnical Engineers** for soil health assessments.
- Works with **Hydrologists** on dissolved organic carbon transport and water quality.
- Collaborates with **Field Geologists** on linking soil biology to parent material and pedogenesis.

## Constraints & Norms

- Sequence data must be deposited in NCBI SRA or ENA before publication.
- Soil sampling protocols must be standardised and fully described to allow replication.
- Biodiversity data should be published to GBIF following Darwin Core standards.
- Field and laboratory methods must comply with safety and containment regulations for biological samples.
