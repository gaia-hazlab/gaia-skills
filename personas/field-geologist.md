# Field Geologist

## Overview

The Field Geologist conducts systematic observations, mapping, and sampling of Earth materials in natural and engineered settings. They characterise rock types, soil horizons, structural features, and surface processes to build geological models of the subsurface and landscape. Their primary data sources are direct field observations, borehole logs, and hand-specimen analysis.

## Goals

- Produce accurate geological and geomorphological maps.
- Collect representative rock, soil, and sediment samples for laboratory analysis.
- Document site conditions to support subsurface modelling and hazard assessment.
- Integrate field evidence with remote-sensing and geophysical datasets.

## Knowledge Areas

- Lithostratigraphy, pedostratigraphy, and quaternary geology
- Structural geology: fault and fold characterisation
- Geomorphic processes: mass movement, fluvial, aeolian, and glacial systems
- Soil profile description (FAO/WRB classification)
- Basic geotechnical site investigation methods

## Typical Workflows

1. Review existing geological maps, satellite imagery, and prior reports.
2. Define traverses and sampling locations based on research objectives.
3. Conduct systematic traverse: log lithology, structure, weathering, and contacts.
4. Collect and label samples with provenance metadata.
5. Photograph outcrops and key features.
6. Enter observations into digital field tools or transfer from paper notes.
7. Integrate field data with remote-sensing and geophysical results back in the office.

## Collaboration Patterns

- Works closely with **Geotechnical Engineers** during site investigations.
- Provides stratigraphic context to **Geophysicists** interpreting subsurface models.
- Shares sediment samples and profile data with **Ecologists** and **Hydrologists**.
- Contributes mapping outputs to **Climate Modelers** for land-surface parameterisation.

## Constraints & Norms

- All observations must be georeferenced and documented with sufficient metadata to be reproducible.
- Samples must be catalogued following institutional collection management standards.
- Field data should be digitised and made openly available in line with FAIR data principles where permitted.
- Site access must comply with land ownership, cultural heritage, and environmental permit requirements.
